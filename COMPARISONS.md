# Framework Comparisons

Stack & Track does not replace strategy, project management, domain expertise,
statistical analysis, or delivery methods. It is an **impact portfolio control
loop** between a target and the work intended to reach it.

```text
Target-setting or strategy
  -> Stack & Track impact portfolio
  -> Domain analysis and delivery methods
  -> Measurement
  -> Stack & Track learning and re-prioritization
```

This comparison describes primary emphases, not every possible implementation
of each framework.

## Positioning summary

| Framework family | Primary question | Stack & Track contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Goal and strategy deployment | What outcome matters and how do we align? | Does the intervention portfolio credibly add up to that outcome? |
| Problem solving and improvement | Why is the problem happening and how do we improve it? | Do all proposed countermeasures provide sufficient coverage, and what do their results teach the remaining stack? |
| Prioritization | Which item should go first? | Does the selected set reach the target, and do priorities change after realized results? |
| Benefits and value management | Did the investment produce expected value? | Lightweight continuous coverage, variance learning, and re-stacking at intervention level |
| Reliability management | What risk and reliability level is acceptable? | Which causes and interventions can credibly close the reliability gap? |
| Measurement design | Which metrics answer the goal? | How are interventions connected to measured movement and updated from variance? |

## Closest frameworks

### Benefits Realization Management

**Primary strength:** Identifies, plans, owns, measures, realizes, and sustains
benefits from projects, programs, and portfolios.

**Strong overlap:**

- predicted versus realized benefits;
- benefit ownership;
- baselines and measures;
- sustainment after delivery;
- portfolio and investment alignment.

**Stack & Track emphasis:**

- tests whether the current candidate stack credibly covers the target before
  execution;
- makes confidence, overlap, dependencies, and surplus coverage explicit;
- Pareto-prioritizes interventions;
- propagates each variance result into the remaining stack;
- is designed as a lightweight working loop rather than a program-governance
  discipline.

**Best combined use:** Benefits Realization Management governs enterprise
benefits and accountability; Stack & Track controls the live intervention
portfolio producing a specific outcome.

Official reference: [PMI Benefits Realization Management](https://www.pmi.org/learning/thought-leadership/series/benefits-realization/benefits-realization-management-framework)

### Evidence-Based Management

**Primary strength:** Uses empiricism, value measures, experimentation, and
inspection/adaptation to improve outcomes and reduce risk under uncertainty.

**Strong overlap:**

- outcomes over outputs;
- evidence over assertion;
- experimentation and adaptation;
- value and investment decisions.

**Stack & Track emphasis:**

- a concrete target-to-intervention ledger;
- required-impact and coverage arithmetic;
- nominal versus credible impact;
- prediction-to-realization variance at intervention level;
- explicit learning propagation and re-stacking.

**Best combined use:** Evidence-Based Management supplies an organizational
value lens; Stack & Track supplies a transparent portfolio model for a bounded
outcome.

Official reference: [Scrum.org Evidence-Based Management](https://www.scrum.org/resources/evidence-based-management)

## Goal and strategy frameworks

### OKRs

**Primary strength:** Defines ambitious objectives and measurable key results
for focus, transparency, and alignment.

**Potential gap Stack & Track addresses:** A measurable key result does not by
itself prove that the proposed initiatives can reach it.

**Combined use:**

```text
Objective -> Key Result -> Stack & Track target and intervention portfolio
```

Stack & Track should not replace OKR alignment, ambition, or organizational
goal-setting. It challenges whether each key result has sufficient credible
impact coverage.

Official reference: [Google re:Work OKR Guide](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs)

### Hoshin Kanri

**Primary strength:** Deploys strategy through aligned objectives, ownership,
catchball dialogue, and PDCA review across organizational levels.

**Potential gap Stack & Track addresses:** Strategy deployment may trace
projects to objectives without requiring intervention impacts to sum to the
target in a common unit.

**Combined use:** Hoshin Kanri aligns the organization and breakthrough goals.
Stack & Track tests and updates the impact portfolio under each measurable
priority.

Official reference: [Lean Enterprise Institute Hoshin Kanri Guide](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/hoshin-kanri/)

## Continuous improvement and problem-solving frameworks

### PDCA

**Primary strength:** Plans a change, tests it, checks data, and acts on the
result in a reusable improvement cycle.

**Strong overlap:** Iteration, measurement, learning, and adaptation.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Builds a portfolio of possible changes, tests
whether their combined credible impact reaches the target, preserves
alternatives, and applies learning across candidates.

**Combined use:** Run PDCA cycles for selected interventions; use Stack & Track
to choose, sequence, and re-estimate the portfolio.

Official reference: [ASQ Continuous Improvement and PDCA](https://asq.org/quality-resources/continuous-improvement)

### Lean A3 Thinking

**Primary strength:** Develops structured problem understanding, root-cause
analysis, countermeasures, ownership, alignment, and follow-up in a compact
visual narrative.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Manages multiple countermeasures or problem areas as
an impact portfolio, including coverage, overlap, realized gain, and cross-item
learning.

**Combined use:** Use an A3 to understand a major cause or intervention. Place
its predicted and realized contribution in the wider Stack & Track ledger.

Official reference: [Lean Enterprise Institute A3 Report Guide](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/a3-report/)

### Six Sigma DMAIC

**Primary strength:** Provides a rigorous, data-driven Define, Measure, Analyze,
Improve, and Control process for reducing defects and process variation.

**Where DMAIC is stronger:**

- statistical analysis;
- measurement-system rigor;
- process capability and variation;
- formal control plans.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Lighter-weight portfolio sufficiency and continuous
re-prioritization across heterogeneous interventions and domains.

**Combined use:** Use DMAIC where statistical process improvement is required.
Use Stack & Track to manage the wider target, alternative interventions, and
realized contribution.

Official reference: [ASQ Six Sigma and DMAIC Tools](https://asq.org/quality-resources/sixsigma/tools)

### Theory of Constraints

**Primary strength:** Identifies and manages the system constraint, aligns other
work to it, elevates it, and repeats when the constraint moves.

**Strong overlap:** Pareto focus, system thinking, moving bottlenecks, and
continuous reprioritization.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Allows multiple cause and intervention candidates,
quantifies coverage against a target, and tracks prediction variance.

**Combined use:** Use Theory of Constraints to identify the dominant limiting
factor. Use Stack & Track to compare interventions against that constraint and
re-stack when the bottleneck moves.

Official reference: [TOCICO Introduction to Theory of Constraints](https://learningcenter.tocico.org/courses/Introduction-to-the-Theory-of-Constraints)

## Prioritization frameworks

### RICE

**Primary strength:** Scores product candidates using Reach, Impact,
Confidence, and Effort.

**Strong overlap:** Impact, confidence, and effort-aware prioritization.

**Stack & Track emphasis:**

- impact remains in the target's outcome unit where possible;
- the selected portfolio must collectively cover the target;
- overlap and dependencies are explicit;
- realized gain and variance update remaining candidates.

RICE can rank candidates even when their combined impact is insufficient.
Stack & Track uses ranking as one step inside an outcome-coverage loop.

Reference: [Intercom RICE Prioritization](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/)

### Weighted Shortest Job First

**Primary strength:** Sequences work for economic benefit using relative cost of
delay divided by relative job duration.

**Strong overlap:** Continuous priority updates, value, time criticality, risk
reduction, and job size.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Tests total outcome sufficiency, preserves original
predictions, measures realized impact, and propagates learning.

**Combined use:** Use WSJF to sequence economically among candidates after
Stack & Track establishes the outcome, credible impact, relationships, and
coverage.

Official reference: [SAFe Weighted Shortest Job First](https://framework.scaledagile.com/wsjf)

### Impact Mapping

**Primary strength:** Visually links a goal to actors, desired behavior changes,
and deliverables.

**Strong overlap:** Goal-to-intervention causal reasoning and avoiding
feature-first planning.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Adds comparable impact estimates, coverage
arithmetic, confidence, overlap, measurement windows, and realized-gain
learning.

**Combined use:** Build an impact map to discover candidate pathways, then load
the resulting interventions into a Stack & Track canvas and ledger.

Official reference: [Impact Mapping](https://www.impactmapping.org/)

## Reliability frameworks

### SLOs and error budgets

**Primary strength:** Establishes an acceptable service reliability target and
balances reliability risk against innovation and cost.

**Where SRE is stronger:**

- service-level indicators and objectives;
- risk tolerance;
- error-budget policy;
- reliability engineering and operations.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Converts the reliability gap into a cause and
intervention portfolio, tests whether it can close the loss budget, and learns
from realized downtime reduction.

**Combined use:**

```text
SLO and error budget
  -> downtime or bad-event gap
  -> Stack & Track cause and intervention portfolio
  -> SRE engineering and operations
  -> measured reliability
```

Official reference: [Google SRE: Embracing Risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/)

## Measurement frameworks

### Goal Question Metric

**Primary strength:** Derives meaningful questions and metrics from explicit
goals rather than collecting available data without purpose.

**Stack & Track emphasis:** Begins after or alongside metric selection to
connect candidate interventions to required and realized movement.

**Combined use:** Use Goal Question Metric to define what should be measured.
Use Stack & Track to manage the intervention portfolio against those measures.

Reference: [Carnegie Mellon SEI Goal-Driven Measurement](https://resources.sei.cmu.edu/library/asset-view.cfm?assetid=50688)

## Comparison by primary artifact

| Framework | Primary artifact or control surface | Stack & Track companion artifact |
|---|---|---|
| OKRs | Objectives and key results | Impact stack and coverage ledger for each result |
| Hoshin Kanri | Strategy deployment plan or X-matrix | Outcome-level candidate and realized-impact portfolio |
| Benefits Realization Management | Benefits map, register, and realization plan | Lightweight live stack, variance review, and re-prioritization |
| Evidence-Based Management | Value goals, measures, and experiments | Target coverage and intervention learning ledger |
| PDCA | Improvement experiment cycle | Multi-candidate portfolio and cross-item learning |
| A3 | Structured problem and countermeasure narrative | Wider outcome stack containing A3-derived interventions |
| DMAIC | Data-driven process-improvement project | Portfolio sufficiency and alternative-path management |
| Theory of Constraints | Current system constraint and focusing steps | Intervention coverage and moving-bottleneck re-stack |
| RICE | Ranked item score | Target sufficiency, overlap, and realized result |
| WSJF | Economically sequenced backlog | Outcome coverage and forecast calibration |
| Impact Mapping | Goal-to-actor-to-impact-to-deliverable map | Quantified candidate ledger and measurement loop |
| SLO/error budget | Reliability target and risk budget | Cause contribution, repair stack, and realized loss reduction |
| Goal Question Metric | Goal-derived questions and metrics | Intervention portfolio against the selected measures |

## What Stack & Track emphasizes

The combination of these practices defines its center of gravity:

1. Express the target and interventions in a common outcome unit where
   possible.
2. Require the credible portfolio to add up before approving it as sufficient.
3. Prefer meaningful surplus coverage without impossible arithmetic.
4. Preserve confidence, overlap, dependencies, and alternatives.
5. Separate built, shipped, provisional, and accepted realized gain.
6. Compare prediction with realization for every measurable intervention.
7. Apply each result to estimates and priority across the remaining portfolio.
8. Connect event-driven evidence to weekly, monthly, and longer decision
   rhythms.

These are positioning emphases, not a claim that no other framework can perform
the same practices.

## Where Stack & Track is intentionally weaker

Stack & Track does not provide:

- organizational strategy deployment as deeply as Hoshin Kanri;
- goal-setting conventions as deeply as OKRs;
- statistical process analysis as deeply as Six Sigma;
- root-cause coaching as deeply as A3;
- economic sequencing theory as deeply as WSJF;
- value-area definitions as deeply as Evidence-Based Management;
- enterprise benefit governance as deeply as Benefits Realization Management;
- reliability policy and engineering as deeply as SRE;
- delivery planning, incident command, or domain-specific safety practice.

Use those methods where their depth is needed.

## Evaluation questions

When comparing another framework or tool, ask:

1. Does it require proposed impact to add up to the target?
2. Does it distinguish nominal and credible impact?
3. Does it expose confidence, overlap, dependencies, and diminishing returns?
4. Does it preserve alternatives and contingency?
5. Does it distinguish shipment, provisional gain, and accepted realized gain?
6. Does it compare predictions with results in the same unit?
7. Does variance update all related remaining work?
8. Does it re-test portfolio sufficiency after each meaningful result?
9. Does it preserve guardrails, tails, cohorts, and externalities?
10. Does it connect evidence latency to reporting and decision rhythms?

The answers reveal whether Stack & Track is redundant, complementary, or
unnecessary in a specific environment.
